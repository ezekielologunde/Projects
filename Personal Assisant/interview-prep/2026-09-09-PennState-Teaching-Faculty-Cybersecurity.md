## Hard questions and strong answers

### "Why teaching faculty and not a tenure-track research line, given you're finishing a doctorate?"
> "I chose a Doctor of Engineering rather than a traditional PhD on purpose. It's an applied, practice-oriented degree built around solving real security problems, not producing a research monograph. That maps cleanly onto a teaching-focused role: I want my applied research to keep showing up directly in the classroom, in labs and assignments, rather than treating teaching and research as separate tracks. Teaching is the part of the work I want to spend my time on."

### "Why leave a community college for Penn State?"
> "I've built real things at Lawson State: a curriculum redesign the department chair now points to as the model, certification-aligned pathways, grant-compliant reporting. What I want next is to do that work at a larger scale, with a program and student population where I can go deeper into a single cybersecurity curriculum instead of also covering general CS, and where the resources and reputation let me build out labs and applied projects further than a two-year college's budget allows."

### "You're currently teaching at Lawson State plus adjunct sections at University of Arizona Global Campus and Adrian College. Can you commit fully to this role?"

My adjunct sections are asynchronous, one course at a time, and I would either complete the current term or hand them off cleanly rather than stack them against a full-time position. A full-time teaching line is the priority; the adjunct work was a way to keep teaching across different formats while at Lawson State, not a parallel career."

### "This role is based in State College. Are you open to relocating?"
> "Yes. I'm in Birmingham now, and I'm open to relocating to State College for the right full-time role. I'd want to talk through timeline once we're further along, but relocation itself isn't a blocker."

### "Walk me through how you'd design a hands-on cybersecurity course."
> "I start from outcomes, not tools: what should a student be able to do at the end that they couldn't do before. At Lawson State that meant mapping labs directly to certification objectives, PenTest+ and CySA+, so a lab on privilege escalation or log analysis is also direct exam prep. I use Kali Linux, Wireshark, and Autopsy for hands-on work, and I build in capture-the-flag style exercises, which I used with high school students in Syracuse to teach ethical hacking and digital forensics. I revise labs on a rolling basis against MITRE ATT&CK updates so the material tracks the current threat landscape instead of going stale."

### "How do you handle academic integrity, especially with AI tools, in a cybersecurity course?"
> "I lean on assessment design rather than detection after the fact. Lab-based and project-based work where a student has to demonstrate a live exploit, walk through a packet capture, or defend a finding out loud is much harder to fake with a generative tool than a written question is. I also grade process, not just output, screenshots, command history, a short explanation of why a step worked, so the assessment is checking understanding, not just a final answer. Where I do use written assignments I design them around a specific dataset or environment unique to that section."

### "How do you adapt teaching for such different student populations, adult learners and career-changers at Lawson State versus traditional undergraduates?"
> "The content adapts less than the pacing and framing does. Adult learners and career-changers need the material tied explicitly to a job outcome and often need flexibility around competing work and family obligations, which is why I've taught across in-person, hybrid, and fully online formats. Traditional undergraduates usually have more time but less real-world context, so I lean harder on scenario-based labs to build that context for them. Either way I'm assessing the same outcomes; I'm just changing how much scaffolding and real-world framing surrounds them."

### "What's your teaching philosophy?"
> "Translate technical practice into something an outcomes-driven learner can actually use. I come from a dual computer science and education background, so I think about pedagogy as much as content: clear learning objectives, hands-on labs over lecture-only delivery, and rubrics that map directly back to those objectives. I also treat curriculum as something that has to keep moving, security doesn't stand still, so a course I taught two years ago shouldn't look identical today."

### "What's a weakness or gap we should know about?"
> "My teaching career so far has been community college, K-12, and online adjunct work, not a large public research university. I don't have direct experience with a program at Penn State's scale or with whatever accreditation or curriculum-committee processes are specific to IST. What transfers directly is that I've already led a full curriculum redesign and sat on governance for accreditation and outcome assessment at Lawson State, so the underlying skill is there; I'd just be learning your specific processes."

### "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a colleague or committee."
> "During the Lawson State curriculum redesign, the committee was split on consolidating several degree tracks into one pathway. Some members were worried about student course load. I brought data: certification alignment, credit-hour compliance, workforce demand, listened to the load concern, and we landed on a stackable pathway that phased the consolidation instead of doing it all at once. The department chair ended up naming me the team's lead voice on cybersecurity curriculum, which came out of that compromise, not around it."

### "How do you stay current given how fast the field moves?"
> "Two tracks. Professionally, I hold CompTIA Security+, and I revise lab content against MITRE ATT&CK updates every term rather than treating a syllabus as fixed. Academically, my doctoral research at George Washington is applied work on AI-driven threat detection, so I'm building and testing real detection pipelines, not just reading about them, and that research becomes lecture and lab material almost immediately."

### "What certifications do you hold, and are you pursuing more?"
> "CompTIA Security+ as of this year, plus the Google Cybersecurity Professional Certificate, Cisco's Introduction to Cybersecurity, and IC3. I'm deliberately building toward certifications that map to what I teach so I can speak to the exam objectives directly with students; PenTest+ or CySA+ would be logical next steps given the pathway I built at Lawson State."
