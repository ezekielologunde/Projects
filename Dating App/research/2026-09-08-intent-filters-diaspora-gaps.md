# Intent, Serious-Dater Filters, and Diaspora Matching: What Exists and Where the Gaps Are

**Date:** 2026-09-08
**Status:** Research complete. Follows the match-cap competitor research in this folder.

## The questions

1. Many people on dating apps are not there to date. They collect likes, grow an Instagram following, browse for entertainment, or want friends and activity partners. What do apps do about that, and should this app have communities?
2. Serious daters care about things mainstream apps treat as optional: what someone does, what they earn, whether they want kids, their religion, their ethnicity, and sometimes the specific tribe, island, or community they come from (a Haitian who wants to date a Haitian, a Yoruba who wants a Yoruba). What is out there for that?
3. Where are the gaps this app can fill?

## Short answer

- **The non-dater problem is real and measured.** Depending on the survey, between a fifth and four fifths of users are on apps for reasons other than meeting someone. The industry's response has been to split those people off into separate products (Bumble BFF, Tinder Double Date, Hinge's community grants) rather than fix the core feed. A capacity-limited app is structurally unattractive to like-collectors because there is nothing to collect. That is a feature to state out loud.
- **The fields serious daters need most are paywalled on the big apps.** Hinge and Bumble put kids, family plans, politics, education, and lifestyle filters behind subscriptions. Ethnicity and religion are free on Hinge. Income is filterable on Match and verifiable on Luxy. Job and education are verified on The League. Nobody verifies intent.
- **Tribe-level and island-level matching exists only in small community apps.** Mainstream apps stop at race-level buckets. Nigerian, Haitian, Caribbean, and South Asian apps go to tribe, state, island, language, community, and in Nigeria's case genotype. Each community has two to five tiny apps, most with a few hundred ratings, and nearly all of them bundle dating with friendship and community because for diaspora users the community is the product.
- **The gap is not a filter.** It is an app that treats non-negotiables as the first screen instead of a paid upsell, lets heritage be expressed at the granularity people actually use, verifies what can be verified, and structurally excludes everyone who is not there to meet someone.

## Thread 1: Who is on dating apps for something other than dating

### The evidence

| Source | Sample | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Pew Research Center, Feb 2023 | US adults who used dating platforms | Major reasons: 44% long-term partner, 40% casual dating, 24% casual sex, 22% make new friends. 19% cite both casual and long-term. |
| Coffee Meets Bagel Dating Realness Report, Nov 2025 | 1,048 US daters aged 21 to 35 | 92% want marriage or a long-term partner. 82% admit swiping without intending to talk or meet. 43% report mixed signals or unclear intentions after matching. 54% name endless swiping as the top frustration. |
| LendEDU, 2017 | 9,761 US college students | Why use Tinder: 44% "confidence-boosting procrastination," 22% hookup, 4% relationship. |
| MTV Insights, 2019, as reported by DatingNews | US ages 18 to 29 | 61% more interested in finding out who finds them attractive than in dating them. |
| Timmermans and De Caluwé, 2017, Computers in Human Behavior | Tinder Motives Scale, 13 motives | Entertainment is the most common motive. Self-worth validation is a distinct, measurable motive. |
| Sumter, Vandenbosch and Ligtenberg, 2017 | Tinder motivations | Six motives: love, casual sex, ease of communication, self-worth validation, thrill, trendiness. Women skew toward friendship and validation, men toward hookups and relationships. |
| Bumble survey, 2025 | Gen Z and millennials | 56% actively seeking new friendships; 52% want tools to build a friend circle. |

The Instagram-growth behaviour is common enough that Tinder's 2023 community guidelines automatically strip social handles from bios and prohibit using the platform to gain followers. Users on Hinge and Bumble report the same pattern and read a handle in a bio as a signal that the person is not there to meet anyone.

### What the industry did about it

| Move | What it is | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Bumble BFF standalone app, Sept 2025 | Friendship split out of the dating app, rebuilt on the acquired Geneva community platform, with groups, chat rooms, and an events calendar | Friend-seekers get their own product instead of polluting the dating pool |
| Tinder Double Date, June 2025 | Pair up with up to three friends and match with other pairs | About 90% of Double Date profiles are under 29; 35% more messages than solo chats |
| Hinge One More Hour, 2024 onward | $1M a year in $25K grants to real-world social groups (hiking clubs, book clubs, HBCU groups) in NYC, LA, and London | Hinge funds community outside the app rather than building it inside |
| Hinge Your Turn Limits, Sept 2024 | No new matches while 8 or more messages await your reply | 20% higher responsiveness |
| BLK (Match Group) | Social feed inside the dating app where users post and interact outside matching | Dating app and social network blended; validation loop stays inside |
| Boo | Dating plus friends plus interest communities in one app, matched by personality type | Over a million users, but reviews complain that basic social actions are paywalled |

The pattern: the big apps concluded that friendship and community are separate products, and that the fix for the validation loop is outside the feed, not inside it.

### What it means for a capacity-limited app

- **The capacity model is a structural repellent for ego-scrollers.** No like counter, no "who liked you" screen, no feed to post in, no social handles. There is nothing to collect. This should be stated in the positioning, not left implicit.
- **A browse cap is still required.** Someone can window-shop profiles for entertainment even if they never match. Viona, 7secs, and WingWo all cap browsing for this reason.
- **On communities: two honest options.**
  - Option A, a community lane inside the app where capacity does not apply (friends, activity partners, events). Every diaspora app does this (Caddi, Rankontre, Moyyo, MyPerson). The risk is that the lane becomes the validation playground and leaks back into dating.
  - Option B, no community lane. Seed the dating pool from a community that already exists (a diaspora association, a church, an alumni network) and point friend-seekers elsewhere. Hinge's grant model is the corporate version of this.
  - Recommendation for launch: Option B. Liquidity comes from an existing community, not the other way round. A community product is a second product, and building it first is how a dating app never launches.

Notes:

- Hinge free filters are gender, location, distance, age, ethnicity, and religion. Everything a serious dater would call a deal-breaker (children, family plans, politics, education, height, substances) is in Advanced Preferences on Hinge+ or HingeX ($49.99 a month). Hinge's own rationale for keeping ethnicity free is to support people of colour looking for shared cultural experience.
- Bumble gives a limited number of advanced-filter uses free and unlimited on Premium. Religion, politics, family plans, and intentions are all in the advanced set.
- Muzz makes ethnicity free and turns it into a hard must-have by default, which is the opposite of the mainstream posture.
- Dil Mil (21,000 ratings, 4.0 stars) is the best-built example of community-level fields: community, faith, background, mother tongue, diet, career, education, raised-in location, intentions. Advanced filtering by community, career, and education requires VIP at $29.99 to $59.99 a month.

### Verification: what can actually be checked

| Claim | Who verifies it | How |
|---|---|---|
| Identity / photos | Hinge, Tinder, Bumble, MyPerson, Diamour | Live selfie against profile photos |
| Government ID | MyPerson (optional NIN), WingWo (planned) | National ID number or document |
| Job and education | The League | LinkedIn import, manual review, or corporate, education, or Doximity email |
| Income | Luxy | Documentation review for a $200K+ badge |
| Genotype (sickle cell) | Diamour (calculator), Genzee Baddies (matching algorithm) | Self-reported, with compatibility logic |
| Wants kids, relationship intent, religion practice | Nobody | Self-reported everywhere |

Intent is the field serious daters care about most and the one no app can verify. The only signals available are behavioural: response rate, whether connections reach a date, whether people end conversations or ghost. Small apps have started surfacing these. WingWo plans ghost indicators, MyPerson runs a coin-escrow "SafeDate" to penalise no-shows, and BLK's 2026 survey found 66.5% of Black singles have experienced "ghostlighting" (someone disappears, then returns acting as if you vanished).

### What serious daters say they want upfront

- BLK 2026 Dating Trends, survey of 4,000+ users: 47.7% raise non-negotiables early, and 86% have covered them by the first few dates. Topics named: faith, political values, financial habits, family goals, relationship timelines. 41.1% say political alignment matters.
- Coffee Meets Bagel 2025: must-haves are emotional connection (99%), physical attraction (97%), shared values (97%), shared interests (97%). Physical attraction is not optional. Hiding photos by default would be a mistake; putting non-negotiables above them is not.

## Thread 3: Ethnicity, tribe, and diaspora matching

### The mainstream controversy

- Grindr removed its ethnicity filter in June 2020 during the Black Lives Matter protests. Scruff followed. Hinge and OkCupid kept theirs. Tinder never had one.
- Hutson, Taft, Barocas and Levy, Cornell, CSCW 2018, "Debiasing Desire": race filters and same-race pairing algorithms reinforce bias. Their recommendations: offer self-description categories beyond race, post inclusive community norms, and do not build algorithms that pair by race.
- The counter-position, voiced by Black women in particular and adopted by Hinge: the filter is a safety and belonging tool for people of colour who want shared cultural background, and removing it mostly helps majority users feel better.

The design line that reconciles both: heritage is something a user says about themselves and chooses to prefer, never something the algorithm infers or optimises on its own.

### Patterns

1. **Granularity is the whole product.** Mainstream apps offer "Black," "Asian," "Hispanic." Community apps offer Igbo, Punjabi, Trinidadian, Efik, and a Local Government Area. A Haitian who wants to date a Haitian cannot express that on Hinge at all. On Caribbean Singles Meet they can.
2. **Diaspora apps bundle dating with friendship and community by default.** Caddi runs three modes. Rankontre puts friendship in its tagline. Moyyo runs meetups. For people far from home, the app is a community first and a dating app second.
3. **Culturally specific seriousness signals exist that mainstream apps have never heard of.** Genotype compatibility is a first-date question in Nigeria because two AS carriers have a 25% chance per child of sickle cell disease. Religion with a practice level (not just a label) is standard on MyPerson and Muzz. "Raised in" versus "born in" is standard on Dil Mil.
4. **Fragmentation and fragility.** Every community has two to five apps. Most have under a thousand ratings. Several run pay-per-week pricing that signals thin revenue. None has a match cap or any answer to choice overload. Muzz shows the ceiling problem: at global scale, the specificity that made it valuable dilutes.

## Gaps this app can look into

Ranked by how directly they extend the capacity-limited concept and how little competition there is.

1. **Non-negotiables free and first.** Hinge charges $32.99 a month to filter on whether someone wants kids. In a not-for-money app, kids, family plans, faith and practice level, politics, relationship timeline, and relocation plans are all free, all set at onboarding, and shown above photos. 47.7% of BLK's users raise these early anyway. The app should do it for them.

2. **Deal-breakers checked before the match, not after.** In a capacity-1 system a wasted match costs the whole slot. Both users' hard non-negotiables should be checked before a like can be sent. No app does mutual pre-screening. Hinge only lets you filter your own view.

3. **Heritage at the granularity people use, on the user's terms.** Self-described fields for ethnicity, tribe or community, island or country of family origin, and languages spoken, each of which the user may mark as "prefer" (soft) or "must" (hard), or leave off entirely. Apply the Cornell guardrails: the user chooses, the algorithm never infers or optimises on it, and the categories are self-authored rather than a fixed race list. This single change lets a Haitian, a Yoruba, and a Gujarati all use the same app without a separate product each.

4. **Culturally specific seriousness fields, optional and explained.** Genotype with a plain-language compatibility note. Religion with practice level. "Raised in" separate from "born in." These are low-cost to add, absent from every mainstream app, and read as "this app understands serious people."

5. **Nothing to collect.** No like counter, no "who liked you," no feed, no posting, no social handles anywhere in the profile, plus a daily browse cap. State it in the positioning. The validation motive accounts for somewhere between 44% and 82% of activity depending on the survey, and this design removes its fuel.

6. **Behavioural intent instead of a self-reported badge.** Every app lets you tick "serious." Only small apps surface what you actually do. A visible, symmetric accountability signal (connections ended with a message versus dropped silently) is cheap in a capacity system because every connection has a clear start and end. This is what WingWo and MyPerson are reaching for with ghost indicators and escrow.

7. **Verify what matters to serious daters, skip what gates the pool.** Selfie identity verification at signup. Optional profession verification through a work or school email, League-style. No income verification: it skews the pool and reads as a status app. Instead, let users state an income band and mark it verified only if they choose to attach proof.

8. **Community as the source, not the feature.** Launch inside one existing community (a diaspora association, a congregation, an alumni network) rather than building groups, events, or a friends mode. That is where the balanced pool comes from, and it keeps friend-seekers and like-collectors out of the dating lane. Revisit a community lane only after the dating loop works.

## What not to do

- No social feed inside the app (BLK). It reintroduces the validation loop.
- No Instagram or social handle field (Tinder now strips them for a reason).
- No paywalled filters, ever. It is the single most-hated pattern across Hinge, Bumble, Dil Mil, and Boo reviews.
- No algorithmic same-ethnicity pairing. Preference is the user's to state, not the system's to infer.
- No hidden photos by default. Physical attraction is a must-have for 97% of daters. Blind-date mode can be an opt-in, as Viona did.

## Open questions

- Which community launches first? Every gap above is easier to test inside one diaspora than across all of them.
- How to word self-authored heritage fields so they work for a Haitian, a Yoruba, a Gujarati, and a fourth-generation American at the same time.
- Whether genotype belongs in a US-launched app or only appears when a user's heritage fields make it relevant.
- Whether a symmetric "ended respectfully" signal can be gamed, and what the abuse cases are.
